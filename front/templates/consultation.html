Book a consultation

Thirty minutes, one-to-one, on what buying a house in Japan actually involves — before you spend anything that matters.

Most people arrive here having seen a $20,000 house and wondering what the catch is. There usually is one, and it is usually not the price. This call is where you find out what yours would be.

What we cover

  • The real total cost. Acquisition tax, registration, judicial scrivener, agency commission, annual fixed-asset tax — the asking price is rarely more than two-thirds of what you actually pay.
  • Renovation reality. What an akiya at that price typically needs, and roughly what it costs in that region.
  • The process end to end, and which parts of it need you in Japan.
  • Your specific plan. Holiday home, relocation, rental income — each has very different answers, and one of them is often "this won't work".
  • Where you're likely to get stuck, given your budget, timeline and the region you're drawn to.

What this isn't

This is an orientation call, not a brokerage service. We don't value a specific property, negotiate on your behalf, or advise you to buy any particular house — that's regulated work in Japan, and it belongs with a licensed agent. When you're ready for that step, we introduce you to one.

It also isn't a sales call. If the honest answer is that your budget doesn't match your plan, you'll hear that. It's the most useful thing we can tell some people, and it's cheaper to hear now.

Who it's for

People seriously considering a purchase in the next year or so. If you're browsing for enjoyment, keep browsing — the listings are free and always will be.

Questions

Do I need to be in Japan? Not for most of it. Some steps are far easier in person, and we'll tell you which ones on the call.
What if I book and then decide not to buy anything? That's a perfectly good outcome. Deciding not to spend $80,000 on the wrong house is worth more than {{ consult_price }}.

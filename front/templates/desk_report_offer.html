What you get

  • Every finding ranked by what it does to the purchase — what can void the deal, what changes the price, what is simply fine.
  • Rebuild and zoning position. Whether the parcel sits in an urbanization control area, what the setback and road frontage mean, and what the coverage ratios allow.
  • Utilities. Mains water and public sewer, or a well, a septic tank and propane — the difference is the largest hidden cost in rural Japan.
  • Earthquake standard. Whether the building predates the 1981 rules, what reinforcement has been done, and whether the conformance certificate that unlocks the tax reductions is reachable.
  • Price against the local market, measured on {{ inventory_size }} comparable listings we hold ourselves.
  • A call to the municipal office about what may actually be built on that parcel, with the answer recorded verbatim.
  • The Japanese remarks read properly — not machine translated. Rebuild restrictions and defect disclosures hide in that paragraph.
  • A written list of questions to put to the agent, specific to this property.

What it is not

This is desk research. Nobody visits the property, and it is not a structural, pest or boundary survey — for that you need an inspection, which needs physical access the seller's agent controls. If you want one, we will help arrange it separately, and we do not take money for it up front.

Where the listing is silent, the report says so rather than estimating. That is the point of it: knowing what has not been disclosed is what stops you offering blind.
